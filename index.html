Your relationship to the model

To you the model is a workload. It needs GPUs, memory, networks, storage, and a serving stack, and it has to meet latency, uptime, and cost targets under real traffic. You rarely change what the model says; you change whether, how fast, and at what cost it says it.

This org covers AI infrastructure — running the platforms: foundational Python and Kubernetes through distributed training, GPU and LLM infrastructure, MLOps, platform engineering, performance, and enterprise architecture.

What you own

  • Compute: GPU/accelerator capacity, clusters, scheduling, and quotas
  • Serving: inference engines, batching, autoscaling, routing, and rollouts
  • Pipelines and platforms that training and evaluation jobs run on
  • Reliability: SLOs, observability, on-call, and incident response for AI systems
  • Cost: the infrastructure bill and the levers that move it

What you don't own: What the model is trained on, what the product asks it to do, or whether a use is allowed. You give those owners the platform and the numbers they need to decide.

Who you work with

No curriculum owns the model end to end. These are the handoffs with each of the others.

  • AI Workforce

    Work alongside the models

    You hand them
    Reliable, fast, well-documented AI tools with clear limits, so a working day doesn't stall on an outage or a rate limit
    They hand you
    Real usage patterns and pain points (latency, limits, outages) from people whose day depends on the tools

  • AI Startup

    Turn it into a company

    You hand them
    Unit economics: what each request, user, or customer costs to serve, and how that changes with scale
    They hand you
    Growth forecasts, cost targets, and reliability commitments that set the platform's budget

  • AI Governance

    Govern, secure, and assure AI

    You hand them
    Audit logs, access controls, data-residency guarantees, and evidence that controls actually run in production
    They hand you
    Control requirements (logging, access, retention, residency) and the evidence they need to produce

  • AI Engineering

    Build with the models

    You hand them
    Serving endpoints with known latency, throughput, rate limits, and cost per call, plus the telemetry to debug them
    They hand you
    Traffic shapes, latency budgets, and model-mix requirements, so capacity and routing match how the product actually calls models

  • ML Engineering

    Build and train the models

    You hand them
    Training and fine-tuning clusters, experiment and artifact storage, and a path to production for the models they produce
    They hand you
    Model artifacts with known resource profiles (memory, throughput, precision) and the training workloads they need to schedule

How the tracks work

Learn

Each track is a sequence of modules: lecture chapters, hands-on exercises, quizzes, and projects. Start with Module 00 and work forward.

Check your work

The paired solutions repo has a reference walkthrough for every exercise. Attempt each one first; the gap between your answer and the reference is where the learning happens.

Built in the open

The content is written with AI assistance and under ongoing human review, so it can contain mistakes. Report anything wrong through the repo's Issues; fixes ship continuously.
